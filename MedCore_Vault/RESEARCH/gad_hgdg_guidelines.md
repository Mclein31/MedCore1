## 1. Executive Summary & Statutory Authority

[FACT] The **Harmonized Gender and Development Guidelines (HGDG)** is the definitive national instrument mandated by the Philippine Government to ensure that gender equality and women's empowerment perspectives are systematically integrated into all stages of program and project development—identification, design, implementation, management, monitoring, and evaluation.

Initially published in 2004 and updated in its **Third Edition (June 2019)**, the guidelines were formulated by the **National Economic and Development Authority (NEDA)**, the **Philippine Commission on Women (PCW)**, and the **ODA-GAD Network** (representing bilateral and multilateral development partners including the World Bank, Asian Development Bank, UNDP, UNICEF, UNFPA, WHO, JICA, AusAID/DFAT, CIDA/Global Affairs Canada, AECID, and ILO).

## 2. Core Concepts & Theoretical Framework

### 2.1 The Sara Longwe Women's Empowerment Framework
[FACT] The HGDG adopts Sara Longwe's *Women's Empowerment and Gender Equality Framework* (UNICEF 1994), which posits that empowerment occurs progressively across five cumulative levels:

1. **Welfare**: Addressing the material and physical well-being of women and girls (e.g., nutrition, health services, maternal survival, basic shelter).
2. **Access**: Ensuring women have equal access to essential resources, services, digital facilities, capital, and institutional entitlements without structural discrimination.
3. **Conscientization**: Sensitizing individuals and organizations to gender biases; challenging the notion that traditional divisions of labor and subordinate female positions are natural or immutable.
4. **Participation**: Guaranteeing women equal, active participation as agents and co-decision makers in program planning, development, implementation, and governance—rather than treating them as passive beneficiaries.
5. **Control**: Achieving a balance of power between women and men, giving women equal control over productive resources, legal decision-making, and the distribution of development benefits.

### 2.2 Gender Needs & Gender Roles
- [FACT] **Practical Gender Needs (PGN)**: Immediate, material needs related to traditional roles and household responsibilities (e.g., affordable medicine, clean water, immediate cash for healthcare) that improve physical conditions without altering unequal power structures.
- [FACT] **Strategic Gender Needs/Interests (SGN)**: Structural and systemic needs aimed at transforming unequal power relations, eliminating institutional discrimination, ensuring legal protections, and securing political/economic self-determination.
- [FACT] **Fourfold Gender Roles**:
  - *Production*: Generation of marketable income, goods, or wage employment.
  - *Reproduction*: Childbearing, childcare, domestic labor, and family health maintenance.
  - *Community Management*: Organizing, mobilizing resources, and community problem-solving.
  - *Leisure*: Rest, recreation, and personal recovery.

## 3. The 10 Core Elements of Gender-Responsive Project Design

[FACT] To ensure projects are gender-responsive, HGDG outlines **10 Core Elements** evaluated through structured checklists:

### Phase I: Project Identification Stage (Elements 1.0 – 3.0)
- **Element 1.0: Involvement of Women and Men in Project Identification (Max: 2.0 pts)**
  - *Item 1.1*: Consultations with female and male beneficiary groups on the problem or issue (0, 0.5, 1.0).
  - *Item 1.2*: Consultation records maintained and sex-disaggregated; inputs integrated into initial design (0, 0.5, 1.0).
- **Element 2.0: Collection and Use of Sex-Disaggregated Data (Max: 2.0 pts)**
  - Collection, classification, and analysis of qualitative and quantitative baseline data disaggregated by sex (0, 1.0, 2.0).
- **Element 3.0: Conduct of Gender Analysis (Max: 2.0 pts)**
  - *Item 3.1*: Analysis of gender gaps, roles, divisions of labor, and access/control over resources (0, 0.5, 1.0).
  - *Item 3.2*: Identification of gender-based constraints and opportunities for participation (0, 0.5, 1.0).

### Phase II: Project Design and Formulation Stage (Elements 4.0 – 10.0)
- **Element 4.0: Gender Equality Goals, Objectives, Outcomes, and Outputs (Max: 2.0 pts)**
  - Articulation of explicit gender equality statements in at least two levels of the project logical framework (0, 1.0, 2.0).
- **Element 5.0: Matching Strategies and Interventions with Identified Gender Issues (Max: 2.0 pts)**
  - Direct alignment between identified gender gaps and project activities, removing barriers to participation (0, 1.0, 2.0).
- **Element 6.0: Gender Analysis of Likely Impacts of the Designed Project (Max: 2.0 pts)**
  - *Item 6.1*: Women and girls comprise at least one-third (33%+) of direct/indirect beneficiaries (0, 0.33, 0.67).
  - *Item 6.2*: Consideration of long-term impacts on women's socioeconomic status and empowerment (0, 0.33, 0.67).
  - *Item 6.3*: Specific strategies implemented to avoid or mitigate adverse impacts on women's welfare and status (0, 0.33, 0.67).
- **Element 7.0: Monitoring Targets and Indicators (Max: 2.0 pts)**
  - Quantitative and qualitative gender equality indicators established to measure status improvements (0, 1.0, 2.0).
- **Element 8.0: Sex-Disaggregated Database Requirement in M&E (Max: 2.0 pts)**
  - Institutional requirement for continuous sex-disaggregated data collection and telemetry within the project M&E system (0, 1.0, 2.0).
- **Element 9.0: Resources and Budget Allocation (Max: 2.0 pts)**
  - *Item 9.1*: Sufficient financial budget earmarked for GAD activities or institutional counterpart funds (0, 0.5, 1.0).
  - *Item 9.2*: Mobilization of gender expertise, designated GAD focal persons, or staff capacity building (0, 0.5, 1.0).
- **Element 10.0: Relationship with Implementing Agency's GAD Efforts (Max: 2.0 pts)**
  - *Item 10.1*: Alignment with partner agency / PCW / LGU GAD plans (0, 0.33, 0.67).
  - *Item 10.2*: Complementarity with other ongoing sectoral or regional GAD initiatives (0, 0.33, 0.67).
  - *Item 10.3*: Institutionalized sustainability and exit mechanisms ensuring post-project continuity (0, 0.33, 0.67).

## 5. Sector-Specific Checklists Relevant to MedCore

[FACT] The HGDG establishes specific sectoral guidelines and checklists directly applicable to MedCore's domain:

### 5.1 Health Sector Checklist (Box 13)
- Health projects must address gender disparities in health outcomes, maternal morbidity/mortality, access to healthcare financing, and healthcare labor forces.
- **Key Health Indicators (Appendix C)**:
  - Sex-disaggregated disease morbidity and hospital admission patterns.
  - Utilization and financial coverage rates of health insurance (PhilHealth, HMOs) disaggregated by sex.
  - Safeguards for female-headed households bearing out-of-pocket medical debt.
  - Support mechanisms for female healthcare workers (nurses, billing clerks) who represent >70% of frontline hospital staff.

### 5.2 ICT Sector Checklist (Box 19)
- Information and Communication Technology projects must address the digital gender divide, ensuring equal access, usability, data privacy, and technological empowerment.
- **Key ICT Requirements**:
  - Accessible, frictionless interfaces operable across low-bandwidth environments without gender exclusion.
  - Strict privacy protections regarding sensitive health and financial data.
  - Sex-disaggregated user engagement metrics and telemetry.

### 5.3 Implementation, Management, Monitoring and Evaluation (Boxes 16 & 17)
- **Box 16 (Project Management)**: Supportive leadership, technical competence in GAD, gender balance across project teams, and adherence to gender-sensitive operating procedures.
- **Box 17 (M&E Checklist)**: Periodic assessment of gender impacts, reporting sex-disaggregated data to regional/national levels, and participatory feedback loops with beneficiaries.
