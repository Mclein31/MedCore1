## 3. Product understanding: the story you are telling

MedCore is an **AI-powered platform for transparent hospital billing and insurance reconciliation** in Philippine healthcare. Its intended role is a financial coordination layer alongside the hospital’s existing systems. It brings hospital charges, eligible adjustments, PhilHealth benefits, private HMO authorization, remaining patient responsibility, and discharge-related financial coordination into a clearer workflow.

The emotional origin is not “we wanted another app.” It is **“we experienced how difficult it was to understand a bill, even when coverage existed.”** A family had benefits but still could not clearly see what they were expected to pay and why. That uncertainty exposed a wider coordination problem affecting patients and staff.

The three problems to communicate are:

1. **Unclear patient responsibility:** the family sees a final number without a comprehensible explanation of charges, coverage, exclusions, and what remains.
2. **Disconnected information:** hospital billing records, PhilHealth information, HMO approvals, professional fees, and departmental clearance work are spread across systems and desks.
3. **Disputes and delays:** patients ask difficult questions while staff recheck records and chase approvals; incomplete or inconsistent claims can create further work.

Do not make patients look irresponsible or staff look indifferent. The conflict is fragmented information and difficult coordination. Give both sides dignity. A billing officer is a person working through a complicated process, not a villain behind a counter.

The prototype demonstrates staff-side orientation, reconciled ledgers, recorded approvals/disputes, clearance workflows, claims/eSOA pre-flight review, and financial analytics; and patient-side bills, coverage, Cora, payment review, recorded receipts, documents, arrangements, and discharge passes.

Keep terminology simple. Say “what remains to pay” before “out-of-pocket responsibility,” “authorization” before “eLOA,” and “electronic statement of account” before “eSOA.” Show the actual labels in the app; use plain language in narration and editorial callouts.
